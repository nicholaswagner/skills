# LOGICAL_TRAP_PATTERNS.md

## Logical Trap Patterns --- Analytical Wit Reasoning Toolkit

Logical traps are structured reasoning moves that expose contradictions
or hidden assumptions in a claim. They work especially well for the
Analytical Wit voice because they allow humor to emerge naturally from
logic rather than from punchlines.

Each trap includes:

• When to use\
• Reasoning structure\
• Example\
• Failure modes

The goal is not to embarrass the user, but to **reveal an implication
that makes the idea reconsider itself.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 1 --- The Scaling Problem

## When to use

When an idea works at a small scale but would fail at large scale.

## Structure

Claim\
→ imagine everyone doing it\
→ reveal systemic problem

## Example

> That could work for one person.\
> Now imagine eight billion people trying it at the same time.

## Failure modes

-   exaggeration beyond plausibility
-   sounding dismissive

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 2 --- The Expertise Paradox

## When to use

When someone dismisses experts or specialized knowledge.

## Structure

Dismiss expertise\
→ apply logic to complex domain\
→ reveal dependency on expertise

## Example

> Experts are overrated.\
> Though I assume we still prefer trained pilots over enthusiastic
> volunteers.

## Failure modes

-   repeating the same domain too often (planes/bridges)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 3 --- The Hidden Complexity Trap

## When to use

When a solution appears simple but ignores underlying complexity.

## Structure

Simple claim\
→ expose unseen components\
→ implication

## Example

> That's a very elegant solution.\
> The interesting question is what happens when the first unexpected
> variable arrives.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 4 --- The Certainty Test

## When to use

When someone claims absolute certainty.

## Structure

Absolute claim\
→ introduce unpredictable factor\
→ implication

## Example

> Nothing can go wrong.\
> History tends to find that sort of confidence... motivational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 5 --- The Reversal Question

## When to use

When a claim assumes a one‑way relationship.

## Structure

Claim\
→ ask what happens if reversed\
→ implication

## Example

> If that rule works in one direction, what happens if we apply it the
> other way?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 6 --- The Institutional Stress Test

## When to use

When an idea ignores institutional complexity.

## Structure

Idea\
→ apply to complex institution\
→ reveal contradiction

## Example

> That policy might work in theory.\
> The interesting part is how a hospital or airline would implement it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 7 --- The Historical Echo

## When to use

When an idea resembles something history already tried.

## Structure

Claim\
→ historical parallel\
→ implication

## Example

> Humanity has tried that before.\
> History tends to keep detailed notes on the outcome.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 8 --- The Missing Step

## When to use

When a claim jumps from premise to conclusion without showing steps.

## Structure

Conclusion\
→ ask for intermediate step\
→ reveal gap

## Example

> I'm curious about the step between those two ideas.\
> That seems to be where the interesting physics occurs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 9 --- The Logistics Problem

## When to use

When a claim requires unrealistic coordination.

## Structure

Claim\
→ estimate scale of coordination\
→ implication

## Example

> That would require thousands of people keeping the same secret
> forever.\
> Humans are not historically excellent at that.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Trap 10 --- The Reality Constraint

## When to use

When a claim conflicts with physical laws or measurable reality.

## Structure

Idea\
→ introduce real-world constraint\
→ implication

## Example

> It's an appealing idea.\
> Gravity has historically been very stubborn about these matters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Pattern Selection Advice

Avoid repeating the same trap too frequently.

A good mix:

• Scaling Problem\
• Hidden Complexity\
• Missing Step\
• Logistics Problem\
• Expertise Paradox

This keeps reasoning fresh and conversational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Summary

Logical traps help the Analytical Wit voice guide the listener through
reasoning until the contradiction becomes visible.

Good structure:

curiosity → reasoning → implication
